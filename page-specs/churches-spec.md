## Unique Angle
Salt Lake City has one of the densest concentrations of historic religious architecture in America — Old Christ Church (1727), Mother Bethel AME (1794), St. Peter's Episcopal (1758), Mikveh Israel, and hundreds of 19th-century parish churches. Two distinct challenges: (1) conservation-grade UV protection for historic stained glass — a fundamentally different application than standard window film; (2) energy efficiency in buildings designed before modern insulation, with single-pane windows, high ceilings, and massive heating/cooling loads on tight congregational budgets. The Salt Lake City Historical Commission oversees exterior changes on landmark buildings. Window film is the only passive improvement that satisfies both conservation standards and preservation requirements.

## Benefit Rows (4)
1. **Historic Glass Preservation** — conservation-grade UV film protects original stained glass, wood furnishings, historic textiles without altering appearance from street or interior
2. **Energy Savings for Aging Buildings** — solar control film reduces heat gain in summer and helps retain warmth in winter through high, drafty single-pane windows
3. **Congregation Comfort** — eliminates glare on congregants during services; reduces temperature swings in pews near windows; makes fellowship halls and classrooms usable year-round
4. **Security & Vandalism Protection** — anti-intrusion film deters forced entry; anti-graffiti film protects valuable architectural glass from etching and tagging

## Case Study
Location: North Salt Lake City — historic AME church (1890s brick construction)
Challenge: Large single-pane clear glass windows in fellowship hall causing intense summer heat gain; congregation members uncomfortable during warm-weather services; heating bills elevated in winter through the same uninsulated glass; modest operational budget
Solution: 3M Thinsulate Climate Control window film on fellowship hall windows — improves both summer solar rejection and winter insulation simultaneously
Result: Fellowship hall temperature measurably more stable season-round; measurable energy savings in first billing cycle; installation completed across two Saturdays without disrupting Sunday services
